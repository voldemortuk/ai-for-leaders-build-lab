Session experience walkthrough · for review
Why we're changing the build
The solo build splits the room. A shared build pulls it together.
▼ Today · Solo hands-on build
Everyone follows one screen, alone
The instructor builds; each leader tries to keep up on their own laptop.
  • Fast builders finish early and coast — it feels too easy
  • Anyone who slips behind quietly disengages — it feels too hard
  • The energy leaks to impatience; the value lands unevenly
  • Leaders leave having watched a build, not owned one
▲ Proposed · Team Build Lab
Small teams build toward a live demo
Leaders build in teams of three, each with an embedded senior engineer, racing to a 3-minute demo.
  • The team absorbs the spread — fast pull, slower learn by doing
  • A Build Coach unblocks instantly; nobody is left falling behind
  • The demo deadline creates energy the solo format never had
  • Every leader leaves with two working agents and a story

How the room works
One instructor up front. Each team has its own senior engineer.
🎤
Front of room
The Instructor
Runs the room, not the keyboards. Leads the common open and the stock-vs-custom contrast, holds the clock, calls the check-in, and MCs the demos. One per session.
🛠️
Inside each team
The Build Coach
A senior engineer from a FAANG+ company (our instructor pool), embedded in one team. Doesn't lecture — pairs, unblocks, and keeps the team shipping. One per team.
👥
The builders
The Team
3 leaders who build together and present together — shared screen, one scenario, one demo at the end. A typical leadership cohort splits into ~5 teams.
⚖️
The Build Coach is the engine of the format — it's what lets a mixed-pace room of senior leaders all reach a working demo. The coach absorbs the gap; the instructor protects the pace.
